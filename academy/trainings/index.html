Trainings

The curriculum.

Two tracks in preparation and integration, built to be taken in order. Either one stands alone if it is the only one you need.

Every track is educational. Coursework covers screening, consent, ethics, the design of a container, and integration. Nothing in these programs is a licence, a medical qualification, or legal permission to do anything. What you do with the education is yours to answer for.
Track one

Foundations in Preparation and Integration

Eight modules, online, at your own pace. Roughly 20 hours of material.

This is the ground floor, and the right course for anyone who wants to understand what responsible practice looks like before deciding anything, on either side of the room.

M 01

What preparation is for

Why the weeks beforehand decide most of the outcome, what preparation can and cannot change, and the difference between preparing a person and reassuring them.

M 02

The screening conversation

Taking a complete medical, psychiatric, and medication history. What each answer changes, which questions people reliably underanswer, and how to keep screening separate from sales.

M 03

Consent, power, and money

What informed consent can honestly mean when the experience cannot be fully described in advance. Touch, capacity, dual relationships, refunds, and the pressure a paid place creates.

M 04

Designing the container

Set and setting as working decisions rather than atmosphere. Roles in the room, duty of care, documentation, the stopping agreement, and what happens when something goes wrong.

M 05

Tradition, lineage, and honesty

Where these practices come from, what is owed to the communities that carried them, and how to work with reverence without claiming a lineage you do not hold.

M 06

What people actually experience

The range of what gets reported, why accounts are hard to give, and the limits of describing any of it in ordinary language. Enough to recognise what you are hearing.

M 07

The first ninety days

Meaning-making without leading, the return to an ordinary week, relapse into old patterns, spiritual bypass, and the practices that reliably help during the months afterward.

M 08

Scope, referral, and when it goes badly

Recognising a clinical presentation, the referral threshold, who to have on your list before you need them, and the professional boundaries that protect both of you.

Track two

Integration Studies

Ten weeks, live online, one evening a week. Built for clinicians.

Therapists, psychologists, coaches, and clergy increasingly have clients who have been through a ceremony and do not know what to do with it afterward. This course is for the person sitting across from them.

Weeks 1–3

What actually happened

Enough grounding in these practices to understand the account your client is giving you, including why it may be hard for them to give it.

Weeks 4–6

Working with the material

Meaning-making without leading, somatic residue, the reassembly of an ordinary sense of self, and the difference between insight and avoidance.

Weeks 7–8

When it goes badly

Prolonged destabilisation, derealisation, spiritual emergency, and the point at which this becomes a psychiatric referral.

Weeks 9–10

Scope, ethics, documentation

Your own professional boundaries, your regulator, your notes, and the conversations that protect both of you.

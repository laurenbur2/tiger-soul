The gray period after Ibogaine

The old pattern is gone, but the future isn't clear yet. If you feel flat, lost, or low and keep asking where do I go from here, you're not broken, and you're not alone.

Depression · The Gray Period · After Ibogaine

Where Do I Go From Here?

Ibogaine can strip away an addiction, a compulsion, or the grip of old trauma in a single long night. But when the thing that ran your life is suddenly gone, a quiet, gray space can open in its place. Many people call it flat, empty, even hopeless. It is one of the most common and least talked about parts of the journey.

You're not alone in it

The Gray Period

The pattern is gone. Now what?

Ibogaine does the hard part of interrupting. What it doesn't do is hand you a new life to walk into. So the weeks after can feel strangely low, like you did the work and still don't know who you are without the thing you left behind.

This isn't a sign it failed. It's the space between an old life and a new one, and almost no one is prepared for how tender that space can feel. What you do with it is what decides whether the change holds.

Medicine of the Gods

A Way Through

Bufo (5-MeO-DMT) can give you a place to begin

Where Ibogaine is long and detailed, Bufo (5-MeO-DMT) is short and whole. In a few minutes it can return you to a felt sense of connection and wholeness, the ground the gray period takes away.

Many people describe it as the moment hope came back, a real starting point and a reason to rebuild rather than just endure. It isn't a fix or a treatment for depression. It's a way to feel like a future is yours to build again.
